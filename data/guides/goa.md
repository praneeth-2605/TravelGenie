# Goa

## Overview
Goa is India's smallest state, on the Konkan coast of the Arabian Sea. It splits roughly into North Goa (busy beaches, nightlife, markets, hostels) and South Goa (quieter, cleaner beaches, upscale resorts). The capital is Panaji (Panjim); the old Portuguese capital, Old Goa, is a UNESCO World Heritage site.

## When to go
- **November to February**: peak season. Dry, sunny, 20–32°C. Prices for rooms rise sharply around Christmas and New Year; book weeks ahead.
- **March to May**: hot and humid (up to ~35°C), fewer crowds, good deals.
- **June to September**: monsoon. Heavy rain, rough seas, many beach shacks close and swimming is often unsafe. Lush and green, great for waterfalls such as Dudhsagar (check that access is open).

## Getting there and around
- Two airports: **Dabolim (GOI)** in the south-centre and **Manohar International (GOX)** at Mopa in the north. Pick the one closer to where you stay; the drive between them is about 1.5–2 hours.
- Trains on the Konkan Railway stop at Madgaon (Margao) and Thivim.
- Getting around: rented scooters are the most common choice (carry your licence and wear a helmet); app cabs are limited, so local taxis and pre-booked drivers are common. Agree fares before you ride.

## North Goa
### Beaches
- **Baga and Calangute**: crowded, lively, water sports, shacks and clubs.
- **Anjuna**: famous Wednesday flea market and cliffside sunset spots.
- **Vagator**: red cliffs, Chapora Fort viewpoint above the beach.
- **Arambol and Mandrem**: laid-back, yoga retreats, drum circles at sunset in Arambol.
### Nightlife
Tito's Lane in Baga is the classic party strip. Saturday night markets run at Arpora in season. Many venues have noise curfews around 10pm outdoors, so late parties move indoors.

## South Goa
- **Palolem**: crescent bay, kayaking, silent-disco parties in season.
- **Agonda**: quiet, turtle-nesting beach; low-key huts.
- **Colva and Benaulim**: wide sandy beaches popular with domestic tourists.
- **Cabo de Rama fort** and **Butterfly Beach** (reached by boat from Palolem).

## Heritage and culture
- **Old Goa**: Basilica of Bom Jesus (holds the remains of St Francis Xavier) and Sé Cathedral.
- **Fontainhas, Panaji**: the Latin quarter with colourful Portuguese-era houses; best explored on foot in the morning.
- Spice plantations near Ponda offer guided tours with a traditional lunch.

## Food
Goan cuisine mixes Konkani and Portuguese influences. Try fish curry rice, prawn balchão, chicken xacuti, pork vindaloo and sorpotel, and bebinca (layered dessert). Feni, made from cashew or coconut, is the local spirit. Beach shacks are the cheapest place for fresh seafood; check the catch and price per weight before ordering.

## Budget tips
- Backpacker: hostels and guesthouses in North Goa; scooter rental and shack meals keep daily costs low.
- Mid-range: boutique stays in Assagao, Siolim or South Goa.
- Prices roughly double in the last two weeks of December.

## Safety
Respect red-flag warnings: currents are strong, especially during the monsoon. Don't ride scooters after drinking. Keep valuables in the room safe on party nights.
