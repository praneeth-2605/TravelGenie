# Tokyo

## Overview
Tokyo is a cluster of distinct neighbourhoods rather than one centre. Shinjuku and Shibuya are the busy west-side hubs; Asakusa and Ueno hold the older, traditional side; Ginza and Marunouchi are upscale; Akihabara is the electronics and anime district.

## When to go
- **Late March to early April**: cherry blossom season. Beautiful and very busy; book early.
- **October to November**: mild, clear weather and autumn leaves. Often the most comfortable time.
- **June to mid-July**: rainy season (tsuyu).
- **Late July to August**: hot and humid, often above 33°C. Festivals and fireworks.
- **Winter**: cold but dry and sunny; views of Mount Fuji are most likely on clear winter mornings.

## Getting around
- An IC card (**Suica** or **Pasmo**, including the mobile versions in Apple Wallet) works on almost all trains, subways and buses, and in convenience stores.
- The JR Yamanote loop line links most major districts.
- Trains stop running around midnight; taxis are expensive after that.
- The nationwide JR Pass rose sharply in price in 2023; it only pays off with several long-distance shinkansen trips. For Tokyo alone, use an IC card or a Tokyo Subway Ticket (24/48/72 hours).

## Neighbourhoods
### Shinjuku
Omoide Yokocho and Golden Gai for tiny bars and yakitori; free views from the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building; Shinjuku Gyoen garden.
### Shibuya
Shibuya Crossing, the Shibuya Sky observation deck (book sunset slots ahead), Harajuku's Takeshita Street and Meiji Shrine next door.
### Asakusa
Sensō-ji temple and Nakamise street; go early morning to avoid crowds. Views of Tokyo Skytree across the river.
### Others
TeamLab Planets (Toyosu) needs advance tickets. Tsukiji Outer Market is good for a seafood breakfast. Yanaka is a quiet, old-Tokyo neighbourhood.

## Food
Ramen, sushi, tempura, tonkatsu and izakaya dishes are everywhere. Convenience stores (7-Eleven, Lawson, FamilyMart) sell good cheap meals. Many popular restaurants need reservations, sometimes weeks ahead; ticket-machine ramen shops don't. Tipping is not customary.

## Day trips
- **Nikko**: shrines and waterfalls, about 2 hours.
- **Kamakura**: Great Buddha and coastal temples, about 1 hour.
- **Hakone**: hot springs (onsen) and possible Mt Fuji views, about 1.5 hours.

## Budget tips
Business hotels and capsule hotels are good value. Lunch sets (teishoku) are much cheaper than dinner at the same restaurants. Many shops offer tax-free shopping for tourists who show their passport.

## Etiquette
Keep quiet on trains and don't talk on phones. Stand on the left of escalators in Tokyo. Carry your rubbish home, as public bins are rare. Tattoos may be refused at some onsen.
