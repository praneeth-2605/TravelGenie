# Bali

## Overview
Bali is an Indonesian island known for Hindu temples, rice terraces, surf beaches and wellness retreats. Areas differ a lot: Seminyak and Canggu (cafés, beach clubs, surfing), Ubud (culture, jungle, yoga), Uluwatu (cliffs, surf breaks), Nusa Dua and Sanur (calm resort beaches).

## When to go
- **April to October**: dry season; sunny and the best time for beaches and diving. July and August are the busiest months.
- **November to March**: wet season, with heavy afternoon showers; greener and cheaper.
- **Nyepi** (Balinese New Year, March/April): the whole island shuts down for 24 hours, including the airport. Plan around it.

## Entry
Most visitors need a visa on arrival or e-VOA, which can be bought online in advance. Bali also charges a tourist levy for foreign visitors, paid online or on arrival. Check current rules before you travel.

## Getting around
Traffic is heavy, especially around Canggu, Seminyak and Kuta. Ride-hailing apps (Grab, Gojek) are cheap but banned from picking up in some areas. Hiring a private driver for the day is common and good value for sightseeing. Only rent a scooter if you're an experienced rider with an international licence.

## Areas and highlights
### Ubud
Tegallalang rice terraces, the Sacred Monkey Forest (keep food and sunglasses out of sight), Campuhan Ridge Walk at sunrise, traditional dance performances in the evening.
### Uluwatu
Uluwatu Temple on the cliff with a Kecak fire dance at sunset; beaches such as Padang Padang and Bingin.
### Canggu and Seminyak
Surf lessons at Batu Bolong, beach clubs, cafés, nightlife.
### Temples
Tanah Lot (sea temple, best at sunset), Tirta Empul (water purification ritual), Lempuyang ("Gates of Heaven", long photo queues).
### Islands
Nusa Penida for Kelingking Beach and manta-ray snorkelling (boats from Sanur); the Gili Islands are a longer fast-boat ride.

## Food
Nasi campur, babi guling (suckling pig), bebek betutu (slow-cooked duck), sate lilit, and fresh juices. Warungs (local eateries) are very cheap; Canggu has many international cafés.

## Etiquette
Wear a sarong and sash at temples (often lent at the entrance). Don't step on the small offerings (canang sari) on the ground. Dress modestly away from the beach.

## Health and safety
Drink bottled or filtered water. "Bali belly" is common, so be careful with ice and raw foods at first. Use reef-safe sunscreen. Strong currents at some beaches; swim between the flags.
